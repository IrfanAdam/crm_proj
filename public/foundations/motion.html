Chapter 2 · Foundations · 06 Motion

Fast for response. Spring for delight.

Motion answers touch in 150ms — anything slower feels broken. Delight gets the spring: overshoot, elastic scroll, and only in visualizations and ceremony. Chrome moves instantly or not at all, and every animation surrenders to reduced-motion.

  • 01

    Respond in 150 — presses, toggles, tab switches ride transition.fast.

    Reject: a 400ms button press — feedback that arrives late is noise.

  • 02

    Spring is earned — overshoot and elastic belong to scroll, gems and ceremony.

    Reject: a springy dropdown — delight spent on chrome is delight wasted.

  • 03

    One easing family — standard in, spring out; elastic only for scroll edges and tilt.

    Reject: a custom cubic-bezier per component — motion must rhyme.

  • 04

    Reduced-motion wins — instant states, no overshoot when preferred.

    Reject: any animation that cannot be switched off — access is not optional.

Orchestra — stagger, not scatter

Lists enter at 40ms stagger. One curve, one timing, sequence does the work — no cascade of durations.

Scroll — elastic edge, spring back

The only chrome that overshoots. Pull past the top and the surface travels 12px on spring 400 (motion.translate.elastic-overshoot) then snaps — a tactile edge, not a bounce. Progress rides scaleX on transition.fast. At prefers-reduced-motion there is no travel.

Choreography

  • →

    Transform + opacity only — layout never moves, only composite properties. No width/height keyframes.

  • ✦

    Enter 150, exit 100, spring for ceremony — chrome fast, delight slow. Exits beat entrances.

  • ◐

    Stagger 40ms, max 5 — after that, group fades. The eye tracks sequence, not scatter.

Snippet
